The idea:
 the system would have three cameras, with a left blind spot camera, right blind spot camera, and rear camera, all streaming through the raspberry pi 5 to the macbook where the actual computer vision and rendering happens
the left and right cameras would constantly monitor the blind spots, and when you activate a turn signal the matching camera view can automatically appear in the xreal glasses with detected vehicles highlighted so you can see what is beside you without relying only on turning your head
the rear camera would constantly monitor traffic behind you and detect vehicles that are getting too close, approaching unusually fast, or entering a rear collision zone, then show a warning or temporarily bring up the rear camera feed
the cameras would also support general object detection for cars, motorcycles, bicycles, pedestrians, cones, stopped vehicles, debris, and other useful road hazards
the system could track detected vehicles over multiple frames instead of treating every frame independently, which lets us determine whether something is staying beside you, moving away, or rapidly closing distance
the bike would connect through can or the diagnostic connector so we can read whatever the motorcycle exposes, including speed, rpm, gear, left and right turn signals, brakes, throttle, wheel information, engine information, and potentially abs data
turn signal state would directly control the hud, so activating the right signal could immediately prioritize the right camera and right blind spot detector while the left signal does the opposite
brake information could also affect the system, so if you brake hard it could pay extra attention to vehicles approaching from behind
an imu in the helmet would measure head movement, acceleration, rotation, and orientation, which we can use for head-aware hud behavior, crash detection, motion tracking, and eventually more stable ar overlays
gps would provide location, speed, navigation, hazard locations, route information, and the ability to associate dangerous events with specific roads or intersections
the xreal glasses would show a minimal adaptive hud rather than constantly covering your vision, with things like current speed, gear, navigation, warnings, nearby vehicle indicators, and temporary camera windows only when they are useful
navigation could show simple directional instructions such as an arrow and distance to the next turn instead of displaying an entire map
the hud would have different states depending on what is happening, so normal riding stays minimal, a turn signal opens the corresponding blind spot information, a rear threat gets priority if something is approaching quickly, and a major hazard temporarily overrides less important information
the system could use the helmet imu to tell roughly where you are looking, so looking toward the right side could prepare or emphasize information from the right camera before you even activate the signal
the macbook would handle the heavy processing including computer vision, tracking, ai, navigation logic, hazard detection, and rendering, while the raspberry pi mainly handles cameras, sensors, motorcycle telemetry, networking, and output to the xreal glasses
the three camera feeds would be sent wirelessly from the pi to the macbook using compressed video, while imu, gps, can, and control information would travel through separate low-latency telemetry streams
the macbook would render the final hud, compress it, send it back to the raspberry pi, and the pi would output it through hdmi and an active hdmi-to-usb-c adapter to the xreal glasses
the cameras themselves could eventually use onboard h264 or mjpeg compression so the raspberry pi mostly forwards the streams instead of having to encode three raw feeds itself
the system could continuously record all three cameras into a circular buffer, so if something happens it can save the previous 20 or 30 seconds along with the following footage instead of only recording after the rider reacts
the event recorder could also save synchronized gps, speed, motorcycle telemetry, and imu information alongside the video, giving you a much better record of an incident than a normal gopro
a button or voice command could manually save an event, while automatic triggers could include a large impact, extreme acceleration, sudden braking, unusual bike orientation, or a detected near collision
crash detection could combine the imu, bike speed, and orientation to recognize a likely crash or bike-down event and generate an emergency event packet containing time, gps position, last known speed, impact magnitude, bike orientation, and the recorded camera footage
for the hackathon we could show that emergency packet on the mac rather than actually contacting emergency services
the system could detect road hazards such as potholes, debris, construction, stopped vehicles, damaged pavement, and potentially slippery or poor road conditions, then associate them with gps coordinates
those hazards could be uploaded anonymously so riders approaching the same location receive warnings before they reach it
multiple riders could therefore create a shared motorcycle hazard network where one rider discovering a problem benefits everyone coming behind them
the system could also build a road quality map by combining repeated imu impacts and gps coordinates, so if many motorcycles repeatedly experience a strong vertical acceleration at the same point it can identify a likely pothole or road defect
that information could be shown on a civic dashboard so cities can see where riders are repeatedly encountering road problems
we could also collect near-miss information rather than only crashes, using combinations of hard braking, sudden steering, close vehicle detection, and rapid relative movement to identify situations where a collision almost occurred
aggregated near-miss locations could create a dangerous-intersection heatmap showing places where riders repeatedly experience risky situations even if crashes have not happened yet
dangerous intersections could then be identified from repeated braking events, collision warnings, close passes, and other rider behavior
the system could detect cross traffic at intersections and warn if another vehicle appears to be moving into the rider’s path
it could also identify vehicles behaving unpredictably by watching for repeated lane drifting, rapidly changing distance, or movement toward the motorcycle rather than trying to guess whether a driver is distracted
the rear camera could distinguish between a vehicle simply existing behind you and one rapidly closing the gap, which makes the warning much more useful than a basic proximity alert
we could eventually estimate approximate vehicle distance and relative velocity so warnings can say that something is 10 meters behind and approaching quickly rather than only saying that a vehicle exists
the system could create small directional indicators around the edge of the hud showing where nearby vehicles are located, then expand the relevant camera view only when something becomes important
group riding could be built in so riders can see the approximate location of the other people in their group, know when somebody was left behind at a light, and receive a warning if another rider unexpectedly stops or crashes
shared group hazards could also propagate immediately between riders so the first person encountering debris or a stopped vehicle warns everyone behind them
navigation could eventually become motorcycle-specific instead of simply finding the fastest route, taking into account road quality, reported hazards, construction, dangerous intersections, lighting, curves, and other rider-specific factors
the route system could compare something like an 18 minute route with several known hazards against a 20 minute route with none and let the rider decide which they want
curve warnings could combine gps route geometry and current motorcycle speed to warn when you are approaching a sharp curve unusually quickly
later we could combine camera information, road conditions, speed, and lean information to make those warnings smarter
the system could eventually recognize temporary events such as crashes, blocked lanes, stalled vehicles, construction, or emergency activity and warn nearby riders
the entire interface would remain contextual, so most of the time you would only see a tiny amount of information and the glasses would become more detailed only when the situation requires it
and the overall concept becomes more than smart motorcycle glasses
it becomes a motorcycle awareness system that sees around the rider, understands what the bike is doing, detects nearby threats, records incidents, helps with navigation, shares hazards with other riders, and produces road safety data that could also be useful to cities
