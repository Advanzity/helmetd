## Video evidence

The Mac sent 450 HEVC frames at 1280x720/30 using its native Metal HUD. Pi
`fpsdisplaysink` observed 412 rendered frames, zero drops and approximately
30 fps before the final poll. This is the observed intermediate counter, not a
claim that all 450 frames rendered. Caps confirmed hardware-decoded DMA-BUF
NV12 delivered directly to Wayland.

After a later interruption, both the foreground receiver and its SSH session
were absent. Pi uptime also indicated a reboot around 16:34 EDT; this task did
not trigger it. The desktop remained active while no receiver listened on UDP
5000. The Mac sender was relaunched independently by the parent task.

The first receiver service attempt failed while joining the running stream:
`Driver did not accept the bitstream parameters`, followed by SIGSEGV. Using
`rtph265depay wait-for-keyframe=true`, a 120 ms jitter buffer, and the direct
DMA-BUF path produced a persistent receiver. Fullscreen was applied after its
first rendered frame. A Wayland screenshot at 1920x1080 confirmed the HUD
covered the desktop. The image is kept locally for inspection, not checked in.

The service started at 16:52:49 EDT. At 8 minutes 10 seconds of uptime it retained
PID 2018 and `NRestarts=0`. The observed hotspot feed had repeated gaps longer than 500 ms;
the last image remained visible. A later counter was 822 decoded / 814 rendered
/ 8 dropped. These sink metrics do not measure network end-to-end latency,
glasses scanout, or sustained 30 fps under the later network conditions. Near
handoff, counters had advanced to 5363 decoded / 5236 rendered / 127 dropped.

A separate two-second test source encoded 60 constrained-baseline H.264 frames
to loopback UDP and exited successfully without interrupting the display. It
proves the software encoder path, not remote receipt or physical capture.
