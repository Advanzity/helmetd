## Video evidence

The Mac sent 450 HEVC frames at 1280x720/30 using its native Metal HUD. Pi
`fpsdisplaysink` observed 412 rendered frames, zero drops and approximately
30 fps before the final poll. This is the observed intermediate counter, not a
claim that all 450 frames rendered. Caps confirmed hardware-decoded DMA-BUF
NV12 delivered directly to Wayland.

After a later interruption, both the foreground receiver and its SSH session
were absent. Pi uptime also indicated a reboot around 16:34 EDT; this task did
not trigger it. The desktop remained active while no receiver listened on UDP
5000. The Mac sender was relaunched independently by the parent task.

The first receiver service attempt failed while joining the running stream:
`Driver did not accept the bitstream parameters`, followed by SIGSEGV. Using
`rtph265depay wait-for-keyframe=true`, a 120 ms jitter buffer, and the direct
DMA-BUF path produced a persistent receiver. Fullscreen was applied after its
first rendered frame. A Wayland screenshot at 1920x1080 confirmed the HUD
covered the desktop. The image is kept locally for inspection, not checked in.

The service started at 16:52:49 EDT. At 8 minutes 10 seconds of uptime it retained
PID 2018 and `NRestarts=0`. The observed hotspot feed had repeated gaps longer than 500 ms;
the last image remained visible. A later counter was 822 decoded / 814 rendered
/ 8 dropped. These sink metrics do not measure network end-to-end latency,
glasses scanout, or sustained 30 fps under the later network conditions. Near
handoff, counters had advanced to 5363 decoded / 5236 rendered / 127 dropped.

A separate two-second test source encoded 60 constrained-baseline H.264 frames
to loopback UDP and exited successfully without interrupting the display. It
proves the software encoder path, not remote receipt or physical capture.

## Black-level correction after the three-panel demo

The wearer still reported a visible background despite exact-black compositor
screenshots. Inspection with `kmsprint -p` showed the HUD directly scanned out
as NV12 on primary plane 83, with `COLOR_RANGE=YCbCr full range` and
`COLOR_ENCODING=ITU-R BT.601`. A black clip from the same Mac renderer/encoder
was tagged limited-range BT.709 (`ffprobe`: `color_range=tv`, `color_space=bt709`).
This mismatch can lift video black and change colors. A screenshot alone does
not validate the direct scanout path or lens transmission.

The receiver now explicitly converts decoded frames to BGRx/sRGB before
`waylandsink`. HEVC decoding remains in hardware. A 10-frame 1280x720 black
clip decoded on the Pi through this conversion had min/max 0/0 in every RGB
channel. After applying the receiver change, `kmsprint` showed an XR24 RGB
primary framebuffer instead of NV12. No HDMI mode, connector range, firmware,
or lens-dimming settings were changed. CPU color conversion adds work; this
is no longer the direct DMA-BUF presentation path described in the earlier test.

Optical improvement still requires wearer confirmation. XREAL Auto Transparency
and electrochromic dimming are separate from video black levels and cannot be
verified from the Pi screenshot.

## October 4: MVP and packet-loss diagnosis

The Mac native HUD now decouples video from object inference, preserves separate
metadata expiry, retries failed/stalled receivers, and marks held images as delayed
after 500 ms (clears them after 1500 ms). Default speed/gear simulation is removed;
`--demo` is explicit. Native route geometry is supplied by the navigation service.
`tools/helmet.sh --pi <address>` starts/reuses HUD and console independently of a
terminal. The fourth camera remains absent.

A live minimize test exposed a definite lifecycle bug: the event loop returned
`window.isVisible`, which exits the whole sender when minimizing the preview.
Minimized/hidden windows now remain active; covered windows skip `nextDrawable`.
The streaming lifetime holds an NSProcessInfo user-initiated, latency-critical
activity. The fixed process stayed responsive for all 120 checks during the
subsequent minimized-window run, even when Wi-Fi itself degraded.

Network measurements on the current iPhone hotspot (2.4 GHz, channel 6):

- An initial 30-second Pi-interface RTP capture observed 1 missing downlink
  packet out of 2039 sequence positions (0.049%), but a 918 ms interpacket gap.
- A later interval observed 158 missing downlink packets out of 2108 positions
  (7.495%), up to 1733 ms gaps, zero probe drops, and 18 Pi UDP send-buffer errors.
  Camera RTP sequence numbers leaving the Pi were contiguous.
- Pi transmit rate fluctuated from 72.2 Mbps down to 5.5 Mbps at roughly -52 dBm.
  Driver TX-failure counters rose sharply. The Mac UDP receive-buffer overflow
  counter remained zero. No kernel hardware errors appeared in the inspected
  interval; Pi temperature was 42.2 C, throttling 0x0, load below 1.
- With the side streams temporarily stopped, the front passed 40/40 freshness
  samples at 144 ms median source age. Its receiver counted 58 lost / 3785 pushed
  RTP packets during that interval. Both side services were restored afterward.

This supports both software lifecycle faults and an unstable wireless link;
packet loss is not resolved. The exact RF/AP cause is not isolated. Compare
against 5 GHz or a wired link before claiming sustained multi-camera reliability.
Wi-Fi power saving is now disabled for the hotspot profile. Front retains its
supported 30 fps mode at 900 kbps; sides use 15 fps at 600 kbps. A temporary front
15 fps experiment failed caps negotiation and was reverted. All uplinks use
roughly three keyframes per second for faster recovery after packet loss.

`tools/network/rtp_probe.py` runs on the Pi with root packet-socket permission and
prints aggregate RTP sequence gaps, timing, capture drops, and kernel UDP deltas.
It does not save video payloads. Native HUD status now exposes per-camera
`rtp_pushed`, `rtp_lost`, `rtp_late`, and `jitter_ms` for matched interval sampling.

Validation: 10 native tests passed before the final lifecycle changes; focused
render, loopback, control, state, and multi-camera checks also passed afterward
(before the one-line minimize predicate fix). Live minimize recovery was then
verified. 47 navigation/control Python checks and 6 audio/API JavaScript checks
passed. A separate native offscreen snapshot verified route-outline rendering.
